United Kingdom: the FCA and PRA rules.

From 18 March 2027, banks, building societies and other firms send an initial incident report, generally within 24 hours of deciding an incident meets the threshold.

  • The threshold is judgement, not a numberReport when you reasonably believe an incident poses a risk to consumers, to safety and soundness or to market integrity.
  • Dual regulated firms assess twiceThe FCA and PRA tests are separate, and each must be recorded.
  • The final report wants root causeAnd lessons learned, within 30 working days.

European Union: DORA.

Banks, payment and electronic money institutions and many other financial entities report major ICT incidents in three stages.

  • Classification decides everythingClients affected, duration, data loss, critical services and economic impact.
  • Suppliers countIncidents at your ICT third parties can be yours to report.
  • A register of suppliersEvery ICT supplier behind critical functions, with exit plans.

Singapore: MAS

A severe and widespread incident must be notified within one hour of discovery, followed by a root cause and impact report within 14 days. It is the fastest clock of the four.

  • The clock starts at discoveryNot at classification. Speed of detection matters.

United States

Banks notify their primary federal regulator no later than 36 hours after determining a notification incident has occurred. Bank service providers must tell their bank customers after 4 hours of material disruption.

  • Service providers have their own clockWhich is why SaaS firms serving banks need one too.
